Research Interests

  • Federated Learning - Federated learning trains machine learning models across many devices or institutions without centralizing raw data. Each participant trains locally and shares only model updates, which keeps sensitive data where it originated. My research covers privacy-preserving aggregation, robustness to heterogeneous and non-IID data, communication efficiency, and defenses against malicious participants. The goal is collaborative AI that is both effective and respectful of data ownership.

  • Agentic AI and Multi-Agent Systems - Agentic AI systems plan, reason, use tools, and take actions toward goals with limited human supervision. When multiple agents interact, they can cooperate, negotiate, or compete, which creates new capabilities and new risks. I study how to design, coordinate, and evaluate such agents. My focus is on reliability, accountability, and secure communication between agents operating in open environments.

  • Robust and Trustworthy AI - For AI to be adopted responsibly, it must be reliable, transparent, and fair. I explore methods that improve model robustness to distribution shift and noise, quantify uncertainty, and provide interpretable explanations for decisions. This work aims to build AI systems that users and stakeholders can understand, verify, and trust.

  • Quantum Cryptography - Quantum computing threatens many of today's widely used cryptographic schemes. Quantum mechanics also enables new forms of secure communication, such as quantum key distribution. I study quantum-secure protocols, post-quantum cryptographic approaches, and their integration with AI-driven systems. This work aims to protect data and communications against both classical and quantum adversaries.

Introduction

Sai Puppala is an Assistant Professor in the Computer Science and Engineering at New Mexico Tech. His research sits at the intersection of Artificial Intelligence (AI), Machine Learning (ML), and cybersecurity, and focuses on developing robust, adaptive, and secure intelligent systems. His current interests include federated learning, agentic AI and multi-agent systems, adversarial machine learning and LLM safety, robust and trustworthy AI, applied AI for safety-critical systems, and quantum cryptography. He earned his Ph.D. in Computer Science and Engineering from Southern Illinois University Carbondale.

Sai has authored over 60 peer-reviewed publications in prestigious journals and top-tier conferences. His work has appeared at highly regarded venues including the NeurIPS, PETS, AAAI, ICMLA, ICWSM, the IEEE/ACM International Conference on Advances in Social Networks Analysis and Mining (ASONAM), and the IEEE 48th Annual Computers, Software, and Applications Conference (COMPSAC). His research has also received media attention for its relevance and impact on technology and society.

Before joining the faculty, Sai worked as a Research Engineering for many fortune 100 companies. In that role, he applied his expertise in Big Data, Machine Learning, and Cloud Services to complex problems in Manufacturing, Transportation, and Healthcare, and contributed to influential projects, including some involving Google Cloud Services. He holds certifications as a Machine Learning, Solutions Architect and Data Engineer. This industry experience gives him a strong foundation in data engineering, data processing, and analytics, and it informs his focus on building scalable, efficient, and secure solutions.

Combining academic research with practical industry experience, Sai approaches problems through data analysis, machine learning model development, and software engineering. He is dedicated to advancing methods that address data security, privacy, and adaptiveness. His goal is intelligent systems that remain resilient, reliable, and trustworthy against emerging threats in an increasingly interconnected world. As an Assistant Professor, he is committed to mentoring students, fostering interdisciplinary collaboration, and pushing the boundaries of knowledge and application in AI and cybersecurity.
